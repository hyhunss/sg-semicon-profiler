# SBF Project Scope

Use these defaults unless the user explicitly provides a different scope.

## Client and purpose

The client is the Singapore Business Federation (SBF). The plugin supports SBF and its sector partners in helping Singapore semiconductor-supply-chain SMEs identify practical US market-entry opportunities. Its outputs are company-level decision support for the broader SBF US semiconductor playbook; they are not the complete 3-to-5-year SBF roadmap.

## Target company scope

The updated capstone scope focuses on Singapore-owned SMEs:

- at least 30% Singapore citizen or permanent-resident equity;
- less than SGD 100 million in revenue; and
- fewer than 200 employees.

Public websites often do not prove ownership or size. Mark those items `Unknown` rather than guessing. Do not reject a company only because public evidence is unavailable, but surface the gap for SBF review. Large multinational companies are outside the SME cohort, though they may appear downstream as US buyers, partners, or route-to-market organizations.

## Industry scope

Prioritize semiconductor supporting industries where Singapore SMEs compete, including equipment and equipment services, precision engineering, testing, assembly, advanced packaging, factory software, cleanroom and facility services, tool installation and relocation, logistics, materials, and systems integration.

Direct semiconductor manufacturing is not the target Singapore SME segment. A fab may still be a US end customer or project signal.

## Geography

Default priority clusters are:

1. Central Texas
2. Arizona
3. New York

California is deprioritized because of cost, not prohibited. Include other US clusters when the capability match or timing signal is clearly stronger. If the user specifies a geography, follow the user's scope.

## Route-to-market ecosystem

Look beyond end buyers. Practical routes can include EPC/EPCM firms, cleanroom contractors, facility integrators, equipment OEMs, approved-supplier programs, economic development organizations, chambers, trade associations, universities, research consortia, and public-sector semiconductor initiatives.

Keep commercial prospects and ecosystem connectors clearly labeled; do not imply that every connector is a direct buyer.

## SBF support stages

Use SBF's four-stage support model when recommending next actions:

- `Learn`: build market understanding and test fit.
- `Lead Generation`: create introductions and validate demand.
- `Land`: support establishment, incentives, partners, and initial market entry.
- `Localize`: deepen local operations, hiring, supplier status, and scale.

Most early-stage SMEs should default to `Learn` unless evidence supports a later stage.
